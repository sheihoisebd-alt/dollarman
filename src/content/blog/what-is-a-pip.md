If you are new to forex, you will hear the word **pip** almost immediately. It is simply the unit traders use to measure price movement.

## What is a pip?

For most currency pairs, a pip is a move in the **fourth decimal place**. If EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0851, it has moved **one pip**.

For pairs that include the Japanese yen, such as USD/JPY, a pip is the **second decimal place**. A move from 150.20 to 150.21 is one pip.

## Why does pip value matter?

The pip itself is just a distance. What matters for your account is **how much money one pip is worth** for the size of your trade:

- 1 standard lot = 100,000 units
- 1 mini lot = 10,000 units
- 1 micro lot = 1,000 units

For a pair quoted in US dollars, such as EUR/USD, one pip on one standard lot is worth about **$10**. On a micro lot it is about **$0.10**.

## A quick example

Say you buy 0.10 lots (one mini lot) of EUR/USD with a stop-loss 25 pips away. One pip is worth about $1, so if the stop is hit, you lose about **$25** (plus costs such as spread).

## Key takeaways

- A pip is a small, standard unit of price movement.
- Pip value depends on the pair and your position size.
- Always know your money risk before you open a trade.
